    AI-Generated Media Check

    Please read before using: Genuine, forensic-grade deepfake detection is a hard, unsolved problem. TITAN does not show an automated "real/fake" verdict for image, voice, or video — an earlier version tried this and gave wrong, misleading answers, which is worse than no answer at all in a real cybercrime case. Instead, all three sections below give an honest, educational red-flag checklist and point to real, purpose-built verification tools. For anything you plan to submit as evidence, request forensic verification through NCCIA/FIA's digital forensics lab.

    Important correction: TITAN previously showed an automated "likely real / likely fake" verdict here. After testing, that verdict was unreliable — the signals a browser can compute (metadata, compression, texture) are too weak to give a trustworthy answer, and a wrong confident answer can genuinely mislead a victim. So instead of a false verdict, here's what a person can actually check by eye, plus real tools built for this.

    ✋ Hands, ears, teeth

    AI images still most often go wrong here — extra/fused fingers, oddly shaped ears, teeth that look too uniform or blend together.

    🔆 Lighting & shadows

    Check if shadows fall in a consistent direction across the whole image, and whether reflections in eyes/glass match the scene.

    🧵 Background & text

    Look at background objects, patterns on clothes, and any text/signage — AI images often warp or garble these even when the main subject looks clean.

    🪞 Skin & edges

    Unnaturally smooth, waxy skin, or a slightly "glowing"/blurred edge where a person meets the background, is a common tell.

    For an actual reliable check, real image-forensics tools include: Hive Moderation, Deepware, Content Credentials Verify (checks for C2PA "AI-generated" labels), or a reverse image search on Google/TinEye to see if the photo exists elsewhere online. For a legal case, request verification through NCCIA's digital forensics lab when filing your complaint.

    TITAN can't run authentic voice-clone detection in this tool — that needs specialized audio-forensics models. Instead, here's what to listen for, plus real tools that do this properly.

    🔊 Flat or robotic tone

    Cloned voices often lack natural breathing pauses, micro-hesitations, or emotional variation across a full sentence.

    🎚️ Pitch/pacing glitches

    Listen for sudden unnatural jumps in pitch or speaking speed, especially at word boundaries.

    🌐 Background mismatch

    Real calls usually have consistent ambient noise; cloned audio often has an unnaturally clean or inconsistent background.

    ⏱️ Scripted urgency

    Voice scams (fake "relative in trouble," fake officer calls) rely on panic to stop you from noticing audio flaws — slow down before reacting.

    For an actual verified check, real audio-forensics tools include: Pindrop, Resemble Detect, or requesting analysis directly from NCCIA's digital forensics lab when filing a complaint.

    Same honesty here — real-time deepfake video detection needs frame-by-frame ML models this browser tool doesn't run. Use this checklist, and escalate to a real tool or NCCIA forensics for anything used as evidence.

    👁️ Unnatural blinking

    Early deepfakes under/over-blink; newer ones are better, but blinking that looks metronomic is still a signal.

    💋 Lip-sync drift

    Watch mouth shapes against audio closely, especially on consonants like "B," "M," "P" — sync often drifts mid-clip.

    🔆 Lighting/shadow mismatch

    Face lighting that doesn't match the rest of the scene, or a soft "halo" edge around the face/hairline, is a common tell.

    🧍 Rigid head/neck motion

    Deepfakes often move the face convincingly but the neck, shoulders, or ears stay unnaturally still.

    For real verified detection: Deepware Scanner, Hive Moderation, or Reality Defender — or request it through NCCIA when you file your complaint.

    Your Rights

    • Filing a complaint is free — no fee is charged.
    • You cannot be refused the right to file an FIR/complaint.
    • Real officers never send an "arrest warrant" over WhatsApp/call, or demand money on a video call.
    • You can ask for your complaint's reference number and follow up on its status.
    • Providing evidence isn't mandatory, but it speeds up the investigation.
